LANTERN & DUST — THE BRIDE THEY SENT BACK — DIRECT PORTRAIT PROMPTS
Generate each prompt once. Save the result as a Flow Ingredient with the EXACT name shown.
These names match the shot list inline tags: Hester | Ezra | Dunmore | Arletta
Global look for scenes: photorealistic cinematic film still, warm firelit/dusk grade, burnt-sienna & gold, shallow depth of field, 16:9.

NOTE: The two leads are both SCARRED — that is the point of the story. Render the
scars as healed, dignified, and human, never grotesque. Young-Ezra (the burned
drifter, 10 years earlier) and young-Hester (the baker's daughter) appear only in
flashback and use generic wording in the shot list, so no separate Ingredients are
needed — use Ezra and Hester for present-day and let the flashback shots describe
"a young" version.

CHARACTER BIBLE (anchors — reuse verbatim in shots for consistency)
=====================================================================

HESTER MERRITT (later HESTER VANCE) — the rejected bride (lead)
Role/arc: a marked, plain mail-order bride publicly rejected at the depot for not
matching her ten-year-old photograph; married by the scarred blacksmith instead;
a mill-keeper's and baker's-daughter head for figures; ruins the greedy man who
threw her away by reading his crooked books; once, long ago, fed and bandaged a
burned young drifter she has forgotten.
ANCHOR: a 33-year-old white woman, a plain steady handsome face with calm gray eyes
and dark hair pinned back simply under a plain bonnet, a smooth pale healed burn scar
along the left jaw and down the neck and across the back of the right hand, straight
backed and quietly unflinching, wearing a modest dust-gray traveling dress with a
high collar

EZRA VANCE — the scarred blacksmith (co-lead)
Role/arc: burned in a smithy fire at 25, drifted marked and hungry, was shown one
great kindness by a bakery girl and carried her brass medal for ten years while he
built himself into the best smith for forty miles; steps forward to marry the
rejected bride, never telling her he has been looking for her face for a decade.
ANCHOR: a 40-year-old white man, very big and heavy through the shoulders, a broad
plain face with a healed burn scar taking the left cheek jaw and neck and pulling
slightly at the eye, deep quiet brown eyes, dark hair and a short beard both flecked
with gray, a curled scarred right hand, wearing a soot-darkened leather blacksmith's
apron over a rolled-sleeve work shirt

EVERETT DUNMORE — the antagonist (the man who sent her back)
Role/arc: the prosperous freight and mercantile man of Poplar Bend; orders a bride by
a flattering photograph and rejects her in the street for her marked face; out of
spite calls the note on the smithy; ruined when the wife he threw away reads his
crooked ledgers aloud to the town.
ANCHOR: a 48-year-old white man, well-fed and pink-faced and pleased with himself,
small shrewd eyes and a fleshy self-satisfied mouth, sandy hair thinning and combed
over, wearing a loud checked waistcoat with a heavy gold watch chain thick as a
bridle rein, soft uncallused hands, an air of catalogue-ordered prosperity

ARLETTA POOLE — the replacement bride (secondary)
Role/arc: the younger, prettier bride Dunmore sends east for after rejecting Hester;
turns out cold, grasping, and loveless; the living proof that a man who buys by the
picture gets a winter.
ANCHOR: a 26-year-old white woman, a conventionally pretty but cold sharp face with
pale calculating eyes and a small hard mouth, glossy dark ringlets, wearing an
expensive fashionable dress and too much jewelry, an air of bored discontent

NOTES
=====================================================================
- Generate 2-3 takes per character and keep the clearest, most front-facing one — that
  locks the face best as an Ingredient.
- Keep the warm-grey background + soft even light identical across all four so they
  blend consistently into scenes.
- THE SCARS ARE THE STORY. Render Hester's and Ezra's burn scars as smooth, healed,
  pale, and dignified — a human mark, not a horror effect. They should read as
  "someone the world misjudged," not as monstrous. This is essential; a grotesque
  render breaks the entire emotional premise.
- The BRASS SAINT CHRISTOPHER MEDAL is the key prop, not a character: in scene prompts
  describe it as "a small worn brass medal on a bit of ribbon." Never show readable
  engraving — text-free doctrine applies to props too.
- FLASHBACK (Ballard's Crossing, ten years earlier): young Ezra is "a starved young
  burned drifter, gaunt, scar fresh and raw" and young Hester is "a plain quick young
  woman of twenty with flour on her apron." Grade flashbacks softer and cooler (a cold
  March) to separate them from the warm present.
- ARLETTA is the visual foil: cold pretty and overdressed against Hester's warm plain
  steadiness. Useful in the "he got a winter" beat and as a thumbnail contrast.
- No minors in this story — no minors-filter risk.
