DESCRIPTION (copy-paste)
=====================================================================

When fever took her husband and left her a penniless widow, the banker who held the mortgage offered Winifred Holloway a Christian mercy: sign her six-year-old son over to be raised rich back east, and the debt would be forgiven. She said no. So he took the boy anyway — as a creditor, with a lawful paper and the sheriff at his side — while two men held her arms in the frozen yard and a coach carried her screaming child away. Then they renamed the boy and told him his mother had given him up.

For twenty years, Winnie scrubbed floors in the town of Sweetwater and let the town call her mad, because every single Sunday she set a second place at her table — a plate, a cup turned upright, and the little chair her husband had carved with a running horse in the back — and waited for a son the whole town swore was never coming home.

This is the story of a mother the world called mad for refusing to stop waiting, the powerful man who stole her child and called it charity, and the Sunday evening a knock finally came at her door. Stay to the end. You have waited your whole life to hear who is on the other side of it.
