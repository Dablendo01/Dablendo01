Hester Merritt came nine hundred miles to marry a man who took one look at her marked face and said, in front of the whole town, "That is not the woman in the picture. Put her back on." She had sent a photograph taken ten years before the fire. The rich man who ordered her never looked long enough to learn what he was throwing away.

But a big scarred blacksmith stepped out of the shadow of his shop and said, "If the lady's not wanted, I'll have her." The town called it pity. It was not pity. Ezra Vance had been carrying a worn brass medal for ten years — and looking, without much hope, for the face of the one person who had ever shown a burned, starving stranger a kindness he could not repay.

This is the story of two people the world judged by a scar and a photograph, the greedy man who sent the best thing in the territory back like spoiled goods, and the secret a husband kept in a box by his bed until his wife found it on the floor by firelight.
