Generate each prompt once. Save the result as a Flow Ingredient with the EXACT name shown.
These names match the shot list inline tags: Winnie | Jonah | Radcliffe | Thomas | Effie
Global look for scenes: photorealistic cinematic film still, warm firelit/dusk grade, burnt-sienna & gold, shallow depth of field, 16:9.

NOTE ON FLASHBACKS: Young Jonah (age 6) and young Winnie (age 28, the happy years)
appear only in flashback and use generic wording in the shot list ("a small boy",
"a young mother"), so no separate young Ingredients are needed — use Jonah for the
present-day adult and Winnie for present-day, and let flashback shots describe the
young versions. Grade all flashbacks softer and warmer (the happy past) or colder
(the winter of the taking) to separate them from the present.

CHARACTER BIBLE (anchors — reuse verbatim in shots for consistency)
=====================================================================

WINIFRED "WINNIE" HOLLOWAY — the waiting mother (lead)
Role/arc: widowed young when fever takes her husband; her six-year-old son is taken
by the banker to settle the debt and shipped east; for twenty years she scrubs floors,
is mocked as mad, and sets his place at Sunday supper — until the day he comes home.
ANCHOR: a 52-year-old white woman, a careworn gentle face lined with patience and old
grief, faded blue-gray eyes still holding a stubborn hope, gray hair pinned back under
a worn dark headscarf, thin work-roughened hands, wearing a plain patched dark dress
and apron under a threadbare gray shawl, quiet unbroken dignity

JONAH HOLLOWAY — the stolen son, returned (co-lead)
Role/arc: taken from his mother at six, renamed, raised loveless and lied to in a cold
eastern house; escapes, reclaims his true name, becomes a circuit judge; uncovers the
truth and comes home twenty years later not knowing if she is alive or ever wanted him.
ANCHOR: a 32-year-old white man, a strong lined handsome face carrying an old buried
sorrow, deep-set thoughtful gray eyes, dark hair going gray at the temples, clean-shaven
with a firm jaw and a small old pale scar at the left corner of the jaw, tall and
dignified, wearing a good plain dark coat and white collar, a held tenderness under a
courtroom composure

MALCOLM RADCLIFFE — the antagonist (the banker who took the boy)
Role/arc: owns the Sweetwater bank and half the town; forecloses on the widow and takes
her son as a creditor, wrapping the theft in Christian charity; prospers for twenty
years as a pillar; ruined at last when the boy returns a judge and opens his books.
ANCHOR: a 68-year-old white man, big and soft and prosperous, a smooth benevolent-seeming
face with cold pale eyes and a mouth used to getting its way, thick silver hair, wearing
fine severe black broadcloth with a heavy gold watch chain, soft uncallused hands folded
piously, an air of respectable Scripture-quoting cruelty

THOMAS HOLLOWAY — the husband (memories only)
Role/arc: Winnie's husband, a farrier and a gentle good man who built their son a little
chair; dies of the fever in the story's first winter; appears only in warm golden
flashbacks of the happy years.
ANCHOR: a 30-year-old white man, a warm open sunburned face with laugh lines and kind
brown eyes, sandy hair, a strong easy farrier's build, wearing a leather farrier's apron
over a rolled-sleeve work shirt, gentle and unhurried, a man who sings under his breath

EFFIE DELL — the faithful neighbor (secondary)
Role/arc: a poor widow herself; the one soul who never mocked Winnie, who left bread on
her step for twenty years and sat with her across the terrible second plate and said
"I know he's coming home"; lives to see it true.
ANCHOR: a 55-year-old white woman, a plain kind weathered face with steady warm eyes and
faint smile lines, gray hair under a plain bonnet, work-worn, wearing a plain worn dark
dress and a brown shawl, humble and faithful

NOTES
=====================================================================
- Generate 2-3 takes per character and keep the clearest, most front-facing one.
- Keep the warm-grey background + soft even light identical across all five.
- THE KEY PROP is the child's chair: "a small handmade wooden child's chair with a
  running horse carved into the back." It is the emotional object of the whole story —
  render it warm and specific and let it recur (empty at the set table; by the hearth;
  pulled up for grandchildren at the end). No readable text anywhere — text-free.
- THE SET SECOND PLACE (plate, cup turned upright, the small chair) is the thumbnail and
  the spine. Compose it to ache: one warm lamp, the empty chair catching the light.
- JONAH's small jaw scar is the recognition key at the climax (the mother finds it by
  touch). Keep it consistent and subtle on his Ingredient so the close-up pays off.
- MINORS-FILTER CAUTION (important for the shot list): young Jonah (age 6) appears only
  in flashback. The happy paddock/bread scenes are safe. THE TAKING is the one risky
  beat — build it from the MOTHER's side: her arms held by two men, her face, the coach
  window, "a small child's hand against the glass," "a small boy led toward a coach."
  Never pair a child-word with a distress-word (no "screaming child" etc.); put the
  anguish on Winnie and the grip on the sheriff and Radcliffe. The reunion is all adults.
- RADCLIFFE reads as a benevolent pillar on the surface and cold underneath — that
  contrast (kind mask, ledger eyes) is his whole character; don't make him a snarling
  villain, make him respectable, which is worse.
