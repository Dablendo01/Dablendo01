LANTERN & DUST — STORY #12 PRODUCTION KIT
"Build the Gallows Now" the Rich Man Said — Before the Jury Ever Spoke for the Widow
Setting: Copper Hollow, 1880s American Old West. Engine: wronged woman publicly condemned
-> hidden truth (the body's evidence) + a protector who finds courage -> public vindication.
Suspense frame: a ticking gallows clock.

=====================================================================
1. CHARACTER BIBLE (5 named — each a Flow Ingredient)
=====================================================================
ADELINE WHITMORE — lead. 26. A poor widow alone on the edge of town, on trial for her life
  for killing the man who attacked her. Quiet, proud, worn plain dress, fading bruises at her
  throat, brown hair pinned back. Broken-then-defiant. The underdog we root for.
CLEMENT SUTTER — the dead man. ~30. Rich, charming in public, a brutal predator in private;
  the town's false "murdered saint." Appears in flashback/reference; DEATH kept off-detail
  (one shot; no gore) — framed as her defense, not spectacle.
AMBROSE SUTTER — antagonist. ~58. Clement's father; owns the bank, the mine, the town. Grief
  turned to ruthless rage; builds the gallows before the verdict. Fine dark suit, cold proud face.
DR. NATHANIEL ASHFORD — protector. ~45. Town doctor who examined the body and the bruises and
  holds the medical proof; must overcome fear of Sutter to testify. Black coat, weary honest eyes.
OPAL MARSH — ally. ~35. Adeline's neighbor; a nobody who cracks the town's silence first and
  pays for it. Work-worn farm wife, plain shawl, brave frightened face.
(Circuit judge appears late, unnamed, as the outside authority Sutter cannot buy.)

4. SEO — DESCRIPTION
=====================================================================
They were building the gallows for Adeline Whitmore before the jury had said a single word.
She could hear the hammers from her cell — because Ambrose Sutter owned the bank, the mine, and
half the mortgages in the county, and Ambrose Sutter had lost his son, and Ambrose Sutter had
promised the town of Copper Hollow that the widow who killed Clement would swing for it. The
trial was a show. The rope was already measured. But there was one thing the rich man did not
know: a quiet country doctor had washed the dead man's body himself, and seen the truth written
on it — a truth that could stop those hammers cold, if only a frightened man could find the
courage to stand up in a town one family owned. This is the story of the autumn a widow they'd
already condemned made a whole town look at the truth about itself.
