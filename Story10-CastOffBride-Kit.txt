JOSEPHINE ELLERY — lead. ~28. Cast-off first wife; secret heiress to a huge northern
  cattle estate. One good plain dress, borrowed dark funeral shawl, brown hair pinned
  neat, calm unafraid face, straight back. The dignified underdog we root for.
CALEB CARRAWAY — villain groom. ~35. Charming, greedy, prices every soul he meets;
  threw Josephine out for a richer match. Good pale wedding coat, oiled charm that
  curdles to ash.
ADELAIDE VESTRY — the second bride. ~20. Banker's daughter; careless, protected,
  not cruel by nature; white wedding dress; her face turns from smug to shaken to
  decent by the end (removes her veil).
AUGUST VESTRY — banker. ~55. Adelaide's father; owns the Larkspur bank; whispered
  the cruel "seat her at the back" idea. Fine dark suit, watch chain, cold ledger eyes.
MARSHAL ISAIAH COBB — reveal figure. ~50. U.S. Marshal for the territory; dust-covered
  from a four-day ride, star on his chest, hard fair weathered face; takes off his hat
  to Josephine. The most powerful man in the room.
BEATRICE — ally. ~65. Boardinghouse widow who takes Josephine in and lends her the
  funeral shawl; sharp, warm, loyal. Gray hair, plain dark dress, kind shrewd eyes.

4. SEO — DESCRIPTION
=====================================================================
When Caleb Carraway threw his first wife out through a closed door on a gray morning,
he told all of Larkspur she was cold, and barren, and a bad bargain. A year later he
found a richer bride — and he sent his cast-off wife an invitation to his second
wedding, with a single chair waiting for her at the very back of the church, behind
the servants, where the whole town could turn and laugh. Josephine Ellery came. In
her one good dress, on the arm of the only friend she had left, and she sat where
they put her and held her head high. And then the church doors opened, and a
dust-covered U.S. Marshal walked in with news in his saddlebag — and he did not take
off his hat to the groom. This is the story of the morning Larkspur found out exactly
who they'd sat at the back.
