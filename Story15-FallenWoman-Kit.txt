"Run the Fallen Woman Out" the Deacon Cried — Then One Rancher Took Off His Coat
Setting: Red Fork, 1880s American Old West. ~37-40 min (target length met).
Engine (Pattern I public shaming): a woman branded "fallen" and paraded to shame her is
covered by one decent man; the real culprit (a respectable deacon) is exposed -> public
vindication. SENSITIVE THEME (seduction/abandonment, unwed mother) — keep VO dignified,
no graphic content; the child is only ever held/loved, never in peril.

=====================================================================
1. CHARACTER BIBLE (5 named + infant)
=====================================================================
FRANCES DEERING (later Frances Stroud) — lead. 19. Orphaned, trusting, ruined by a
  respectable man and branded "fallen"; carries her baby with unbroken dignity. Plain worn
  dress, soft open face gone wary. The underdog we root for.
WYATT STROUD — protector. ~35. A quiet weathered widowed rancher who covers Frances with his
  coat and takes her in; asks nothing, gives everything; marries her. Big, plain, decent.
ROLAND PYKE — antagonist. ~45. The town's finest hypocrite; freight owner, church deacon;
  the real father who branded Frances to save himself; a serial predator (letters expose a
  prior victim). Handsome, pious, cold.
DELPHINE COOMBS — antagonist (mob-leader). ~50. Iron-cornered church matron who leads the
  parade of shame; needs a fallen woman to stand on. Prim, loud, righteous.
THE FIRST GIRL — Pyke's earlier victim (unnamed / name her at record if wanted); veiled, then
  unveils to testify. ~30.
THE BABY — Frances's infant son; always held/loved, NEVER paired with peril (minors filter).

her. It was a Sunday, just after church, and the good people of Red Fork lined both sides of
that dusty street to shame the fallen woman and drive her out — an unwed mother, a lesson to
their daughters — while the deacon who led the cry from the pulpit walked at the very front of
the crowd. But not one righteous soul in Red Fork knew the truth of who had really fallen, and
who had done the pushing. And one quiet rancher standing on the boardwalk had done the arithmetic
the whole town refused to do. This is the story of the morning he stepped down into the street
and took off his coat — and the dead man's letters that finally named the real sinner.
