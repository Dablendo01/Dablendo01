Setting: Pine Hollow, 1880s American Old West. ~36-38 min (target length met).
Engine (Pattern G, frontier-Cinderella / stolen inheritance): an orphaned heiress is turned

1. CHARACTER BIBLE (5 named)
=====================================================================
NELL AINSLEY (later Nell Weld) — lead. 17. Orphaned heiress made a hidden drudge for 6 years;
  plain cast-off dress, smudged, but quick, kind, well-read, unbroken spirit (secret loft
  book, sings when alone). The underdog we root for.
ISAAC WELD — the good man. ~22. The big cattleman's only son; quiet, watchful, unimpressed by
  paraded daughters; finds Nell singing in the barn and loves her before he knows she's rich.
AUGUSTA BRACKEN — antagonist. ~45. Nell's aunt; the driving cruelty; hides Nell so she'll never
  marry and claim the estate. Handsome, cold, respectable, controlling.
HORACE BRACKEN — antagonist. ~48. Nell's uncle and corrupt guardian; drained her estate;
  cowardly under his bluster; arrested for the theft.
LAVINIA BRACKEN — the favored daughter. 16. Pretty, accomplished, empty; raised as her mother's
  tool; wears Nell's stolen dresses; redeemable (Nell offers her a place at the end).
(County-seat lawyer appears late, unnamed, as the honest outside authority.)

4. SEO — DESCRIPTION
=====================================================================
On the morning of the biggest dance Pine Hollow had seen in ten years, Nell Ainsley was on her
knees in the barn, mucking out the stalls, because her aunt had ordered her kept out of sight
until it was over. She was seventeen, plain, in a cast-off dress, worked from dark to dark and
hidden from every eye — the family secret, kept small, kept down. What Nell did not know was
that everything: the house, the good bottomland, the money buying her cousin's ribbons — all of
it belonged by rights to the girl on her knees in the stalls. And a quiet rancher was about to
wander out back looking for his horse, and hear a girl singing in the dark. This is the story of
the buried heiress they hid in a barn — and the one man who had the ears to catch her worth.
