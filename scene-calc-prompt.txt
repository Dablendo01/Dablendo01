LANTERN & DUST — SCENE-SYNC PROMPT (paste into the Claude Project)
Purpose: turn a timed scene sheet into finished, VO-matched image prompts in one
pass, so each image already lasts exactly as long as the narration under it.

=====================================================================
THE WORKFLOW (three steps, no manual nudging)
=====================================================================
STEP 1 — Get real timing.
  After recording the VO, export captions as .srt from your voice tool
  (ElevenLabs, CapCut, Descript, Whisper, or YouTube auto-captions).
  If you have no SRT, use the plain script + the total runtime instead.

STEP 2 — Run the scene calculator (scene_calculator.py).
  SRT:    python3 scene_calculator.py captions.srt --out scenes.csv
  Script: python3 scene_calculator.py script.txt --seconds 2491 --out scenes.csv
  It splits the VO into scenes cut at sentence boundaries, each ~10s, and gives
  every scene an exact IN/OUT timecode and duration. One image per scene.
  (Tune with --target: lower it, e.g. --target 8, for shorter/faster cuts.)

STEP 3 — Feed the scene sheet to this prompt to write the image prompts.

=====================================================================
PASTE THIS PROMPT (with the scene sheet) INTO THE PROJECT
=====================================================================
"""
You are the shot designer for Lantern & Dust. Below is a timed scene sheet from
one story's voiceover — each scene has a number, an IN/OUT timecode, a duration,
and the exact narration spoken during it. Write ONE image prompt per scene that
illustrates what is being narrated in that exact window.

RULES (follow all):
- One image per scene. The image must depict the moment/beat in that scene's
  narration — not the scene before or after. This is what keeps image and VO in
  sync with zero editing.
- Text-to-Image, photorealistic cinematic film still, 1880s American frontier,
  warm burnt-sienna & gold grade, shallow depth of field, 16:9.
- PERIOD ANCHOR ON EVERY LINE (required): each prompt must END with an explicit
  era tag so it renders old-west even when pasted alone with no file header.
  Append verbatim to every prompt: ", 1880s American Old West frontier,
  period-accurate frontier clothing and props, no modern objects, no
  contemporary clothing". Interiors, close-ups, and hands/props shots are the
  ones that go modern without it, so NONE may be skipped.
- Put character NAMES inline at the START of the prompt, matching the story's
  Flow Ingredient names exactly (e.g. "Abel, Amos — ..."). Scenery-only scenes
  get no name tag.
- Bake the emotion into the prompt (grief, contempt, tenderness, dawning dread).
- NO em-dashes inside prompts (the automator breaks on them). NO timecodes, no
  style tail, no readable text/signs/engravings (text-free doctrine).
- MINORS SAFETY: never pair a child-word with a distress-word; describe a child
  as calm/eager/sleeping and put danger on the adults. Deaths = peaceful-sleep
  framing (eyes closed, lamplight, a hand held), closed coffins only.
- Keep each prompt one flowing sentence, concrete and camera-ready.

OUTPUT FORMAT — exactly this, nothing else:
SCENE 001 | 0:00:00.000-0:00:10.2 (10.2s)
NAMES — the image prompt text

(blank line between scenes)

At the end, list any scene whose duration is under 5s or over 16s so I can decide
whether to split or hold it.

Here is the scene sheet:
[PASTE THE scene_calculator.py OUTPUT HERE]
"""

=====================================================================
WHY THIS ELIMINATES THE EDITING
=====================================================================
Old way: fixed 10-second holds. A sentence that takes 13s spills onto the next
image; a 6s sentence leaves an image hanging. You nudge all 200+ by hand.
New way: each image's duration IS the narration length under it, cut at a
sentence boundary. In the editor you drop image N at its IN timecode and it's
already aligned. The only images that ever need a manual look are the few the
calculator flags as very short or very long.

TIP FOR ANIMATION SHOTS (first ~15 scenes): the same scene sheet drives them —
animate scenes 1 to 15, then hold the rest as stills. Each animated clip is
rendered to its scene duration, so the cold open also lands on the VO.
